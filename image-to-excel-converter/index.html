Image to Excel Converter - Turn a Table Photo into a Real Spreadsheet

In short: drop in a photo, scan or screenshot of a printed table and this free tool rebuilds it as a real Excel (.xlsx) file - rows, columns and all - entirely inside your browser, so your image is never uploaded anywhere.

Someone sends you a staff list, a price table or a results sheet as a picture, and Excel wants it as data. Retyping it is slow and one wrong digit ruins it. Choose an image below, the tool reads the table, shows it back to you as an editable grid so you can fix any cell, and then downloads a proper .xlsx (or .csv) file. This version reads English; the first conversion downloads the English OCR data (about 11 MB) from this website, and your browser caches it for next time.

Private by design: the OCR engine (Tesseract.js) is stored on this website and runs 100% in your browser. Your image is never uploaded to a server, never stored and never seen by us - close the tab and everything is gone.

How to use the Image to Excel Converter

  1. Choose an image of a printed table - a screenshot, scan or straight, well-lit photo. You can also drag and drop the file onto the panel, or paste a screenshot with Ctrl+V.
  2. Press Convert to Table and watch the progress bar. The first time, your browser downloads the English language data (about 11 MB); after that it is cached and conversions are quick.
  3. Check the table. Click any cell to fix a mistake, delete a wrong row with its ✕ button, or add a missing row. This quick proofread is what makes the final sheet trustworthy.
  4. Download the .xlsx file (opens in Excel, Google Sheets and LibreOffice, header row in bold) or a plain .csv.

How it works

OCR (optical character recognition) scans the shapes in your image and matches them against a trained model of printed characters, noting the exact position of every word. This tool uses Tesseract.js, the browser version of the open-source Tesseract engine, with its English LSTM model - the same engine as our Image to Text Converter. It then rebuilds the table from the word positions: words on the same line are grouped into rows, the horizontal layout is analysed to detect columns, and every word is placed into its cell. Finally a spreadsheet library (SheetJS) running in your browser writes the result as a genuine .xlsx file.

Honest limits: a clean, straight image of a printed table - screenshot, scan or sharp photo - converts best, with most cells exactly right. A skewed or shadowy photo, very cramped columns, merged cells or handwriting will produce some wrong cells. That is exactly why the recognised table is shown to you fully editable before download: thirty seconds of checking beats retyping the whole sheet.

Worked example

A colleague photographs a printed staff duty list - 45 names with ID numbers, designations and branches - and sends it over WhatsApp. Instead of typing it all, you run the photo through this tool: the names, IDs and branches land in their own columns, you fix two names the photo blurred, delete the empty row at the bottom, and download a working Excel sheet in about a minute.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is my image uploaded to a server?

No. The OCR engine (Tesseract.js) and its English language data are stored on this website and run entirely in your browser. Your image never leaves your device, and nothing is sent to us or to any third party.

How accurate is the conversion?

On a clean, straight image of a printed table - a screenshot, a scan or a sharp, well-lit photo - most cells come out exactly right. Skewed photos, shadows, merged cells and cramped layouts are harder, which is why the recognised table is fully editable before you download: click any cell, fix the few mistakes, then export.

Can it convert handwritten tables?

Not reliably. The OCR engine reads printed and typed text; handwriting usually comes out garbled. For handwritten sheets, a printed retype or our Image to Text tool plus manual cleanup will serve you better.

My table is spread over several photos. What should I do?

Convert one image at a time and download each sheet, then copy the rows together in Excel or Google Sheets. Each download is a standard .xlsx file, so combining them takes a minute.

Is it really free?

Yes - free, with no sign-up and no page limits. The conversion runs on your own device, so there is no server cost to pass on to you.
